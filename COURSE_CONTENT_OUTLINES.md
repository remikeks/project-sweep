# SWEEP Academy Course Content Outlines

## Purpose

This is the production-content blueprint for the current 30-course catalogue. It is not final teaching material. Each course outline is the source brief for its learner guide (DOCX/PDF), slide deck, video lessons, downloadable activities, and ParaLearn CBT assessment.

## Standard course package

Every course should ship with:

- a learner guide in DOCX and accessible tagged PDF;
- one slide deck, with speaker notes and an accessible PDF export;
- three short lesson videos (8–15 minutes each), with captions and transcripts;
- a practical worksheet or case activity;
- a source/reference list appropriate to the delivery country and professional standards;
- a ParaLearn CBT assessment blueprint: 15–20 scored items, a 70% pass mark, feedback by objective, and item metadata; and
- upload metadata: school, course code, title, module order, asset type, language, version, author, reviewer, approval status, accessibility status, storage URL, checksum, and publication date.

Do not publish clinical, legal, child-protection, or safeguarding guidance without subject-matter review and localisation for the relevant jurisdiction.

## School of Child & Family Welfare

### 1. Foundations of Child Protection

**Outcome:** Recognise protection concerns, respond safely, and follow the local reporting pathway.

1. **Child protection foundations** — child rights, safeguarding principles, roles, and the distinction between concern, risk, and harm.
2. **Recognising and responding** — indicators of abuse/neglect, trauma-informed communication, documentation, immediate safety, and boundaries.
3. **Reporting and case coordination** — mandated reporting, referral pathways, confidentiality limits, multi-agency coordination, and reflective case scenario.

**Assets:** safeguarding concern checklist; documentation template; reporting-pathway diagram; scenario: disclosure by a child. **CBT focus:** recognition, first response, documentation, referral, ethics.

### 2. Family Preservation & Kinship Care

**Outcome:** Plan safe, strengths-based support that preserves family relationships whenever appropriate.

1. **Family-centred assessment** — strengths, risks, culture, family networks, and child voice.
2. **Preservation and safety planning** — in-home services, measurable safety plans, crisis support, and review points.
3. **Kinship care practice** — identifying kin, caregiver assessment, support needs, contact planning, and permanency review.

**Assets:** family-network map; safety-plan worksheet; kinship assessment case. **CBT focus:** assessment, safe preservation decisions, kinship support, review criteria.

### 3. Permanency Planning & Reunification

**Outcome:** Develop timely, child-centred permanency plans and assess reunification readiness.

1. **Permanency principles** — stability, attachment, timelines, child participation, and concurrent planning.
2. **Reunification practice** — readiness indicators, parent support, supervised contact, risk reassessment, and transition planning.
3. **Alternative permanency** — guardianship, adoption, long-term care, cultural continuity, and case review.

**Assets:** permanency timeline; reunification-readiness tool; concurrent-plan case. **CBT focus:** child-centred decision making, concurrent planning, readiness evidence.

## School of Mental Health & Clinical Practice

### 4. Biopsychosocial Assessment

**Outcome:** Conduct a collaborative, ethical whole-person assessment and formulate priority needs.

1. **Assessment framework** — engagement, consent, presenting concerns, biological/psychological/social domains, and strengths.
2. **Risk and formulation** — mental-state observation, suicide/self-harm and safeguarding screening, cultural humility, and formulation.
3. **From assessment to plan** — goals, referrals, documentation, review, and simulated intake.

**Assets:** assessment template; risk-screening prompt sheet; anonymised intake case. **CBT focus:** domains, risk escalation, formulation, documentation.

### 5. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy Foundations

**Outcome:** Explain core CBT concepts and use basic, supervised CBT-informed tools appropriately.

1. **The CBT model** — links among situations, thoughts, emotions, bodily responses, and behaviour.
2. **Core tools** — agenda setting, thought records, cognitive reappraisal, behavioural activation, and homework.
3. **Ethical application** — collaboration, cultural adaptation, limits of competence, monitoring, and referral.

**Assets:** thought-record worksheet; behavioural-activation planner; practice vignette. **CBT focus:** cognitive triangle, tool selection, collaborative practice, scope limits.

### 6. Crisis Stabilization & Safety Planning

**Outcome:** Respond to acute distress using de-escalation, risk assessment, and collaborative safety planning.

1. **Crisis recognition and triage** — acute risk, protective factors, emergency thresholds, and local escalation routes.
2. **De-escalation and engagement** — communication, environment, grounding, and maintaining professional boundaries.
3. **Safety planning and follow-up** — warning signs, coping actions, contacts, means-safety discussion, documentation, and warm handover.

**Assets:** safety-plan template; escalation decision tree; crisis role-play. **CBT focus:** triage, de-escalation, safety-plan elements, referral thresholds.

## School of Substance Use & Addiction Recovery

### 7. Screening & Brief Intervention (SBIRT)

**Outcome:** Use screening and brief-intervention principles to identify and respond to risky substance use.

1. **Substance-use continuum** — stigma-free language, risk factors, protective factors, and screening purpose.
2. **Screening and feedback** — validated-tool principles, consent, interpretation, and brief feedback.
3. **Brief intervention and referral** — motivational conversation, harm reduction, referral matching, and documentation.

**Assets:** screening workflow; brief-intervention script; referral map. **CBT focus:** SBIRT sequence, interpretation, harm reduction, referral.

### 8. Motivational Interviewing

**Outcome:** Use the spirit and core communication skills of motivational interviewing in appropriate settings.

1. **MI spirit** — partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation, autonomy, and avoiding the righting reflex.
2. **OARS skills** — open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries, and listening for change talk.
3. **Planning change** — responding to ambivalence, eliciting commitment language, goal setting, and referral.

**Assets:** OARS cue card; conversation transcript annotation; role-play rubric. **CBT focus:** MI spirit, OARS, change talk, responses to resistance.

### 9. Recovery-Oriented Systems of Care

**Outcome:** Coordinate strengths-based, long-term recovery supports across systems.

1. **Recovery orientation** — recovery capital, person-defined goals, peer support, and anti-stigma practice.
2. **Coordinated support** — housing, employment, family, health, legal, and community supports.
3. **Sustaining recovery** — relapse-prevention planning, continuity of care, outcome monitoring, and lived-experience leadership.

**Assets:** recovery-capital map; support-network plan; systems-coordination case. **CBT focus:** recovery principles, peer roles, whole-system coordination.

## School of Gerontology & Aging Services

### 10. Aging in Place & Independent Living

**Outcome:** Assess and support safe, dignified independent living for older adults.

1. **Person-centred ageing** — autonomy, capacity, culture, rights, and social determinants.
2. **Home and community assessment** — falls, accessibility, medication/health supports, isolation, and caregiver capacity.
3. **Coordinated ageing-in-place plan** — services, technology, family collaboration, review, and safeguarding.

**Assets:** home-safety checklist; service-plan worksheet; home-visit scenario. **CBT focus:** risk factors, autonomy, supports, review.

### 11. Dementia Care & Caregiver Support

**Outcome:** Apply person-centred approaches to dementia support and caregiver wellbeing.

1. **Understanding dementia** — common presentations, communication, stigma, and preserving identity.
2. **Daily support and responsive care** — environment, routines, distress behaviours, consent, and family partnership.
3. **Caregiver support** — burden, respite, education, crisis planning, and referral.

**Assets:** communication guide; caregiver-burden conversation prompts; care-plan case. **CBT focus:** person-centred care, communication, caregiver supports.

### 12. Elder Abuse Prevention & Long-Term Care Advocacy

**Outcome:** Recognise elder abuse and advocate for safety, rights, and quality care.

1. **Forms and indicators of abuse** — physical, emotional, sexual, financial, neglect, and institutional harm.
2. **Safe response** — private inquiry, capacity/consent considerations, documentation, reporting, and emergency action.
3. **Advocacy in long-term care** — rights, complaints, quality monitoring, family involvement, and systems improvement.

**Assets:** abuse-indicator guide; reporting flowchart; long-term-care advocacy case. **CBT focus:** indicators, safe response, reporting, resident rights.

## School of Community & Macro Practice

### 13. Community Needs Assessment

**Outcome:** Plan and conduct a participatory assessment that identifies needs, assets, and priorities.

1. **Assessment design** — purpose, stakeholders, ethics, inclusion, and mixed-method choices.
2. **Collecting and interpreting evidence** — surveys, interviews, focus groups, asset mapping, and data quality.
3. **Prioritisation and communication** — ranking needs, validating findings, reporting back, and action recommendations.

**Assets:** stakeholder map; survey/interview template; community-profile case. **CBT focus:** participatory methods, ethics, assets versus deficits, prioritisation.

### 14. Community Organizing & Coalition Building

**Outcome:** Mobilise communities and partners around shared, achievable goals.

1. **Organising foundations** — power analysis, community leadership, issue selection, and trust building.
2. **Coalition formation** — partners, roles, governance, communication, conflict management, and inclusion.
3. **Campaign action** — strategy, tactics, mobilisation, evaluation, and sustainability.

**Assets:** power map; coalition charter; campaign-planning worksheet. **CBT focus:** organising principles, coalition roles, ethical mobilisation.

### 15. Program Development & Evaluation

**Outcome:** Design a feasible community programme and measure whether it works.

1. **Programme design** — needs-to-goals chain, theory of change, target groups, and feasibility.
2. **Implementation planning** — activities, budget assumptions, partnerships, risk management, and inclusion.
3. **Monitoring and evaluation** — indicators, baseline, data collection, learning loops, and reporting.

**Assets:** logic-model template; indicator bank; evaluation-plan case. **CBT focus:** theory of change, outputs/outcomes, indicators, evaluation ethics.

## School of Medical & Health Social Work

### 16. Hospital Discharge Planning

**Outcome:** Coordinate safe, person-centred transitions from hospital to home or community care.

1. **Discharge-risk assessment** — medical, psychosocial, functional, financial, caregiver, and housing factors.
2. **Transition coordination** — patient/family participation, interdisciplinary roles, medication and appointment continuity.
3. **Follow-up and readmission prevention** — warning signs, referrals, documentation, and handover.

**Assets:** discharge checklist; transition-of-care plan; complex-discharge case. **CBT focus:** risk factors, coordination, patient participation, follow-up.

### 17. Chronic Illness & Adherence Support

**Outcome:** Support people living with chronic conditions to overcome practical and psychosocial barriers to care.

1. **Living with chronic illness** — illness burden, stigma, social determinants, self-management, and strengths.
2. **Understanding adherence barriers** — beliefs, cost, access, literacy, side effects, mental health, and family factors.
3. **Collaborative support plan** — goal setting, education, reminders, referrals, monitoring, and advocacy.

**Assets:** barrier-assessment tool; self-management plan; adherence-support scenario. **CBT focus:** barriers, collaborative goals, adherence supports.

### 18. Interdisciplinary Collaboration in Healthcare

**Outcome:** Work effectively and ethically in healthcare teams while protecting the social-work perspective.

1. **Team-based care** — roles, scope, referrals, shared goals, and social determinants of health.
2. **Communication and coordination** — case conferences, handovers, documentation, advocacy, and constructive disagreement.
3. **Ethics and quality improvement** — confidentiality, consent, equity, burnout prevention, and reflective practice.

**Assets:** role-mapping exercise; SBAR-style handover guide; team-conflict scenario. **CBT focus:** roles, communication, confidentiality, advocacy.

## School of School Social Work

### 19. Student Support & Attendance

**Outcome:** Identify attendance barriers and coordinate supportive, non-punitive interventions.

1. **Understanding absenteeism** — attendance patterns, barriers, equity, safeguarding, and early warning data.
2. **Student and family engagement** — assessment, strengths, home-school partnership, and culturally responsive communication.
3. **Intervention and monitoring** — individual plans, referrals, attendance review, and escalation pathways.

**Assets:** attendance-review form; student-support plan; family-engagement case. **CBT focus:** barriers, early intervention, safeguarding, monitoring.

### 20. Behavioral & Emotional Support in Schools

**Outcome:** Apply trauma-informed, positive, and inclusive support for students’ behavioural and emotional needs.

1. **Behaviour as communication** — development, trauma, neurodiversity, culture, and avoiding punitive assumptions.
2. **Tiered supports** — classroom strategies, individual plans, crisis response, and referrals.
3. **Collaboration and review** — student voice, caregiver partnership, teacher consultation, documentation, and outcome monitoring.

**Assets:** behaviour-support plan; teacher consultation guide; classroom case. **CBT focus:** trauma-informed practice, tiers of support, inclusive response.

### 21. School-Wide Systems & Policy

**Outcome:** Influence school climate, procedures, and policy using social-work and equity principles.

1. **School systems and climate** — discipline, inclusion, wellbeing, safeguarding, and data-informed problem identification.
2. **Policy analysis and improvement** — stakeholders, equity impact, implementation planning, and communication.
3. **Whole-school change** — restorative practices, multi-tiered systems, evaluation, and sustaining change.

**Assets:** policy-review checklist; climate-data dashboard brief; school-improvement case. **CBT focus:** systems thinking, equity impacts, implementation.

## School of Criminal Justice & Forensic Social Work

### 22. Foundations of Forensic Social Work

**Outcome:** Explain social-work roles and ethical responsibilities in justice-related settings.

1. **Practice contexts** — courts, corrections, policing, victim services, child protection, and community programmes.
2. **Assessment and intervention** — psychosocial reports, risk/needs principles, advocacy, and trauma-informed care.
3. **Ethics and boundaries** — dual roles, confidentiality limits, informed consent, documentation, and cultural humility.

**Assets:** justice-settings map; ethics decision aid; forensic-assessment vignette. **CBT focus:** roles, confidentiality limits, ethics, trauma-informed practice.

### 23. Diversion Programs & Restorative Justice

**Outcome:** Describe suitable diversion and restorative approaches and their safeguards.

1. **Alternatives to incarceration** — eligibility, aims, equity, community-based support, and evidence.
2. **Restorative justice principles** — voluntary participation, accountability, repair, victim safety, and facilitator neutrality.
3. **Case coordination** — referral, service plans, monitoring, completion, and avoiding net widening.

**Assets:** eligibility flowchart; restorative-conference preparation guide; diversion case. **CBT focus:** eligibility, consent, victim safety, programme safeguards.

### 24. Reentry Planning & Community Reintegration

**Outcome:** Build coordinated plans that support safe reentry after detention or incarceration.

1. **Reentry needs** — housing, employment, health, identification, family, stigma, legal obligations, and risk.
2. **Planning before release** — assessment, goal setting, records transfer, benefits, family preparation, and warm referrals.
3. **Community follow-up** — continuity of care, peer support, crisis response, progress review, and advocacy.

**Assets:** reentry-plan template; service-navigation map; pre-release case. **CBT focus:** needs assessment, pre-release planning, continuity of care.

## School of Disaster Response & Crisis Intervention

### 25. Disaster Preparedness & Response Planning

**Outcome:** Support organisational and community readiness before and during a disaster.

1. **Disaster-risk context** — hazards, vulnerability, capacity, inclusion, and ethical preparedness.
2. **Preparedness planning** — roles, communications, evacuation/shelter supports, safeguarding, and continuity plans.
3. **Response coordination** — rapid needs assessment, information management, referrals, staff safety, and review.

**Assets:** preparedness checklist; rapid-needs-assessment tool; response-tabletop exercise. **CBT focus:** risk, preparedness roles, coordination, inclusion.

### 26. Psychological First Aid

**Outcome:** Provide humane, practical first support after distressing events within professional limits.

1. **What PFA is and is not** — principles, safety, dignity, culture, consent, and avoiding forced disclosure.
2. **Look, listen, link** — immediate needs, calming support, active listening, information, and practical assistance.
3. **Referral and self-care** — high-risk signs, protection issues, referral, documentation, and helper wellbeing.

**Assets:** PFA field card; referral decision tree; disaster-survivor scenario. **CBT focus:** PFA principles, Look–Listen–Link, referral thresholds.

### 27. Long-Term Disaster Recovery Support

**Outcome:** Support equitable long-term recovery and community resilience after an emergency.

1. **Recovery over time** — displacement, livelihoods, grief, service disruption, inequity, and community leadership.
2. **Case and community support** — housing, benefits, mental-health referrals, child protection, and participation.
3. **Resilience and evaluation** — recovery indicators, feedback, preparedness learning, and sustainable partnerships.

**Assets:** recovery-needs matrix; community-recovery case; resilience indicator worksheet. **CBT focus:** long-term needs, equity, coordination, resilience.

## School of Social Policy & Advocacy

### 28. Policy Analysis Fundamentals

**Outcome:** Analyse a policy’s intent, implementation, stakeholders, and effects on communities.

1. **Policy basics** — policy cycle, actors, evidence, values, and social-work roles.
2. **Analytical framework** — problem definition, target population, benefits/burdens, feasibility, and equity.
3. **Communicating analysis** — policy brief structure, recommendations, evidence use, and ethical advocacy.

**Assets:** policy-analysis canvas; policy-brief template; local-policy case. **CBT focus:** policy cycle, equity analysis, evidence, recommendations.

### 29. Legislative Advocacy & Coalition Building

**Outcome:** Plan ethical, evidence-informed advocacy to influence legislation and public decisions.

1. **Legislative landscape** — how bills move, decision makers, committees, timing, and jurisdiction.
2. **Advocacy strategy** — power mapping, coalition roles, messages, meetings, public engagement, and ethics.
3. **Campaign delivery** — briefing decision makers, mobilising constituents, monitoring outcomes, and adaptation.

**Assets:** legislative tracker; advocacy message matrix; coalition action plan. **CBT focus:** legislative process, strategy, coalition practice, ethical lobbying.

### 30. Using Data to Drive Policy Change

**Outcome:** Turn responsible practice and community data into persuasive policy evidence.

1. **Data foundations** — quantitative/qualitative data, data quality, consent, privacy, and disaggregation.
2. **Finding the story** — trend interpretation, inequity, visualisation, lived experience, and limits of inference.
3. **Policy communication** — evidence brief, charts, recommendations, stakeholder presentation, and evaluation.

**Assets:** data-quality checklist; policy-chart workbook; evidence-to-message case. **CBT focus:** data ethics, interpretation, equity, evidence communication.

## ParaLearn CBT blueprint

ParaLearn should remain the assessment system of record. For every course, publish a versioned assessment package containing:

1. course ID and content version;
2. learning-objective IDs mapped to each item;
3. 15–20 single-best-answer questions, with plausible distractors and rationales;
4. at least two scenario/application items;
5. pass mark, attempt policy, randomisation rules, time limit, and feedback policy;
6. CBT API callback signature/secret configuration, attempt ID, score, pass/fail, item-level outcome summary, and timestamp; and
7. reconciliation rules for retries, delayed callbacks, duplicate callbacks, and revoked results.

SWEEP should never determine a pass from browser-submitted answers once ParaLearn is live. SWEEP should create an attempt, redirect/embed ParaLearn as appropriate, then award progress/badges only after a verified server-to-server callback or a verified result fetch.

## Content production workflow

1. Curriculum lead approves this outline and local regulatory references.
2. Subject-matter expert drafts the learner guide and activity.
3. Instructional designer creates the slides, script, and CBT blueprint.
4. Video producer records captioned lessons from the approved script.
5. Reviewer checks factual accuracy, safeguarding/clinical scope, accessibility, and branding.
6. Content manager uploads or programmatically imports approved assets, attaches metadata, and publishes a version.
7. ParaLearn assessment is published against the same course/content version.
