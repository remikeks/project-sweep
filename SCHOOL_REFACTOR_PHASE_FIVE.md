## Research and interpretation

The batch combines IFSW ethics, the IPCC synthesis, adopted curriculum documents, official WHO guidance and four OA research sources. R8 preserves the previously checked IJSDS Port Harcourt contextual study. New research includes a selected Finnish ecosocial-practitioner study (online 2025, issue 2026), the 2025 Ambio systematic thematic participation review and a September 2026 African pollution review. Source records state design, evidence period, licence, use and limitations.

The participation review covers English-language Scopus articles from 2015–2020 despite its 2025 publication. It is not presented as a current intervention-effect estimate. The African review includes mixed observational designs, has narrow searches and inconsistent design counts, and references protocols/preprints; no pooled local effect or proven heat-respiratory interaction is used in teaching. The Finnish study involved fourteen already-interested practitioners and does not represent Nigerian prevalence. The IJSDS study supplies contextual questions, not causal effects or national estimates.

WHO's 2026 heat guidance is used through its actual 26-page executive-summary file, not a claim to have reviewed the full 272-page guidance. Its European setting requires authorised local adaptation. The WHO air-quality publication overview supports questions about measurement context; no numerical threshold, individual diagnosis or Nigerian legal limit is reproduced. Clinical treatment, engineering, sampling and regulatory decisions remain with appropriate qualified roles. Exercise budgets use fictional units, not real prices or financial commitments.

SCImago's own indexed results report Ambio's 2025 SJR Q1 and a Q1 entry for Current Environmental Health Reports. Direct ranking-table access was restricted; category/year details for the latter and JCR quartiles were not independently downloaded. These limitations remain explicit. No quartile is claimed for IJSDS or Journal of Social Work, and no inference is made that the hybrid journals are fully OA. Article licences and journal ranking are separate from methodological validity.

Official PDFs, metadata and retrieval receipts are held privately. The direct Springer HTTP-200 response was a challenge page, not article evidence; indexed publisher methods and limitations were reviewed instead. The Sage direct request and an institutional-copy availability attempt failed, while the indexed publisher full text supplied the relevant method and limitation evidence. No access restriction was bypassed. The retrieval script labels detected challenge pages explicitly. The source README records access limitations rather than equating a successful download with appraisal.
