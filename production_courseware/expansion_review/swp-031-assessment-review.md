## Module 1: Digital inclusion and service design

Outcome: Choose an accessible service route with the person, based on their access, preferences, privacy and support needs.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-031-M1-P1**: A shared phone can create access and privacy barriers even when a person technically owns or borrows a device.

- **SWP-031-M1-P2**: Test a proposed channel with people affected by access barriers before scaling it; a staff-only test misses real usage conditions.

- **SWP-031-M1-P3**: An alternative route should provide a comparable opportunity to use a service, not a punitive or indefinitely delayed option.

- **SWP-031-M1-P4**: Ask about functional access needs instead of inferring digital ability from age, disability or education.

- **SWP-031-M1-P5**: Measure service completion and abandonment by route, using proportionate data and user feedback; logins alone do not demonstrate inclusion.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-031-Q01

A referral service assumes everyone with a phone can attend a private video appointment. A person shares one phone with several relatives. What should the worker establish first?

- A. Whether relatives can attend every appointment
- B. Whether a private, affordable and accessible channel is available to that person **[Key]**
- C. Whether the phone is the newest model
- D. Whether video appointments are faster for staff

Rationale: Phone access alone does not establish privacy or usability. Device age, staff convenience and family attendance do not resolve the person's access needs.

#### SWP-031-Q02

An online intake form works well on staff laptops but residents report difficulty using it. Which pilot would best test inclusion?

- A. Observe willing users with varied access needs completing representative tasks **[Key]**
- B. Ask the software supplier to confirm that it is modern
- C. Count how many staff members prefer the new design
- D. Launch it everywhere and review only completed forms

Rationale: A representative task-based pilot can expose barriers among people unable to complete the form. Successful submissions alone exclude those who dropped out.

#### SWP-031-Q03

A learner cannot afford mobile data. The service offers an offline appointment only after all online applicants have been seen. What is the main design problem?

- A. An offline route automatically prevents confidentiality
- B. Digital and offline routes must use identical equipment
- C. The alternative route introduces unequal access through avoidable delay **[Key]**
- D. The learner must acquire a better phone first

Rationale: Comparable access concerns the opportunity and timeliness of service, not identical technology. An alternative should not disadvantage users merely for being offline.

#### SWP-031-Q04

A worker assumes an older resident cannot use a digital service. What is the best next step?

- A. Exclude the resident from digital options
- B. Arrange a family proxy without consulting the resident
- C. Ask the resident about preferences and support needs, then offer suitable choices **[Key]**
- D. Require the resident to demonstrate every phone function

Rationale: Individual preferences and practical needs are relevant; age-based assumptions and unnecessary tests can remove choice.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M1-P4

#### SWP-031-Q05

A digital service reports twice as many logins after redesign. Which additional information would best test whether access improved?

- A. The total number of emails sent by the supplier
- B. The colour most frequently used in the interface
- C. Completion and drop-off patterns across access routes, alongside user feedback **[Key]**
- D. The number of devices purchased for managers

Rationale: Login volume does not show whether people obtained support. Completion, drop-off and feedback help reveal remaining barriers.

## Module 2: Information governance and secure practice

Outcome: Design a proportionate information-handling plan and identify when local governance advice is required.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-031-M2-P1**: Before collecting information, specify its purpose, who needs it, how it is protected and which locally approved retention rules apply.

- **SWP-031-M2-P2**: Consent discussions should explain the channel's use, foreseeable privacy limitations and available alternatives in understandable language.

- **SWP-031-M2-P3**: An approved channel must still be used appropriately: check the intended recipient and share only necessary information.

- **SWP-031-M2-P4**: A suspected information incident needs prompt action through the organisation's incident route; preserve relevant facts and follow containment instructions.

- **SWP-031-M2-P5**: Retention periods and exceptions require applicable policy and legal review; neither indefinite retention nor immediate deletion is a universal rule.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-031-Q06

A new appointment form requests full family histories although it only schedules a call. Which revision best fits its purpose?

- A. Ask relatives to submit their records as well
- B. Collect the contact and access details necessary to arrange the call **[Key]**
- C. Require extra identity documents for every enquiry
- D. Retain the full history in case a future project needs it

Rationale: The task is scheduling. Unrelated histories add exposure without helping that task; additional data requires a justified purpose.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M2-P1

#### SWP-031-Q07

A person signs a generic form but says they do not know who can read messages sent through the service. What should happen next?

- A. Ask the person to find the platform's technical specification
- B. Explain access and relevant risks, check understanding and revisit the communication choice **[Key]**
- C. Continue collecting sensitive details before discussing access
- D. Treat the signature as proof that every risk was understood

Rationale: A signature does not resolve an expressed misunderstanding. The worker should support an informed choice before proceeding with the channel.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M2-P2

#### SWP-031-Q08

A worker is about to send a sensitive attachment through an approved system. Two contacts have the same surname. What is the safest next step?

- A. Verify the intended recipient and the necessary contents before sending **[Key]**
- B. Send it to both contacts to avoid delay
- C. Move the attachment to a personal account
- D. Assume the first autocomplete suggestion is correct

Rationale: An approved platform does not prevent a wrong-recipient error. Recipient verification and proportionate disclosure address the immediate risk.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M2-P3

#### SWP-031-Q09

A worker discovers that a case attachment went to the wrong recipient. What is the best initial response within role?

- A. Wait until the next annual review to disclose it
- B. Erase the activity record before anyone sees it
- C. Use the incident-reporting route promptly and follow authorised containment steps **[Key]**
- D. Ask colleagues to keep the event informal

Rationale: Reporting enables assessment and containment. Concealing the event or destroying its record can obstruct an appropriate response; notification duties need local advice.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M2-P4

#### SWP-031-Q10

A team asks how long all client messages must be kept. What is the appropriate basis for deciding?

- A. The applicable retention schedule and legal requirements, verified with the responsible lead **[Key]**
- B. A universal seven-day deletion rule for every service
- C. The default setting of whichever phone is used
- D. Unlimited retention because storage is cheap

Rationale: The draft cannot prescribe a universal duration. The decision depends on the record purpose and applicable requirements.

## Module 3: Telepractice, AI, and professional accountability

Outcome: Recognise unsuitable digital practice and maintain accountable human judgement when using AI assistance.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-031-M3-P1**: Telepractice needs a suitability and privacy check plus an agreed response to connection loss or urgent concerns, adapted to the service and local procedures.

- **SWP-031-M3-P2**: Check AI-generated statements against reliable case information; an AI draft is not a verified record or an accountable decision-maker.

- **SWP-031-M3-P3**: Do not enter identifiable case material into an unapproved AI service; use authorised tools and information-governance procedures.

- **SWP-031-M3-P4**: Review differential effects and error patterns before relying on an automated recommendation; average performance can hide exclusion.

- **SWP-031-M3-P5**: A professional should be able to explain and document the reasons for a decision, including how an AI suggestion was checked and when advice was sought.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-031-Q11

At the start of a remote appointment the worker learns the person cannot speak privately. What is the best response?

- A. Continue because the appointment has already started
- B. Require the person to disclose the concern in the shared room
- C. Record the whole household to document the barrier
- D. Discuss a safer suitable channel or time and apply the service's risk procedure if needed **[Key]**

Rationale: The absence of privacy changes suitability. A safer arrangement should be considered without forcing disclosure or adding intrusive recording.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M3-P1

#### SWP-031-Q12

An AI summary adds a referral that is absent from the verified notes. What should the worker do before using it?

- A. Keep it because a later worker might make that referral
- B. Ask the person to confirm a referral without disclosing the uncertainty
- C. Accept it because the text sounds professionally written
- D. Check the original information and correct or remove the unsupported statement **[Key]**

Rationale: Plausible wording is not evidence. The worker remains responsible for the accuracy of any record they use.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M3-P2

#### SWP-031-Q13

A free AI tool asks a worker to paste a named case history to improve a draft. The organisation has not approved the tool. What should the worker do?

- A. Use an approved workflow and seek governance advice before sharing case information **[Key]**
- B. Remove only the first name and assume the rest is anonymous
- C. Share it and ask permission afterwards
- D. Paste it because the account is password-protected

Rationale: A password or partial name removal does not establish an authorised, safe disclosure. Approval and data handling must be considered first.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M3-P3

#### SWP-031-Q14

A triage tool performs well overall but frequently misroutes requests written in a minority language. What needs review?

- A. Whether affected users can be removed from the evaluation
- B. Only whether staff like the dashboard
- C. Only the overall accuracy because it is higher than last year
- D. The unequal error pattern and safer routing options for affected users **[Key]**

Rationale: The subgroup pattern identifies a specific access risk. An overall average can conceal it, and excluding affected users would hide the problem.

Teaching link: SWP-031-M3-P4

#### SWP-031-Q15

A worker rejects an AI recommendation after finding a factual error. Which record best supports accountability?

- A. A statement that the computer made the decision
- B. The relevant error, verified information, final reasoning and any consultation **[Key]**
- C. No record because AI was not ultimately followed
- D. Only the final AI confidence score

Rationale: A concise reasoning record explains the human decision and its basis; the score alone does not.
