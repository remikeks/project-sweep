## Module 1: Climate risk, inequity, and rights

Outcome: Distinguish a climate hazard from unequal exposure, vulnerability and capacity to respond.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-032-M1-P1**: A hazard map does not fully describe social risk: exposure, housing, livelihood dependence and capacity to respond can differ within the same area.

- **SWP-032-M1-P2**: Do not describe communities only through deficits; identify local knowledge, mutual support and resources alongside constraints.

- **SWP-032-M1-P3**: Displacement-related planning must consider access to livelihoods, services, relationships and meaningful participation; moving people is not itself proof of a just outcome.

- **SWP-032-M1-P4**: Participation records should identify whose perspectives are missing, not equate attendance with representation.

- **SWP-032-M1-P5**: Separate observed local impacts from explanations that need specialist evidence; avoid attributing every individual event to climate change without support.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-032-Q01

- A. Only whether the forecast uses the same colour for both areas
- B. Only which area pays more property tax
- C. Whether all residents can be assigned an identical risk score
- D. How living conditions and response capacity change the consequences of the hazard **[Key]**

Rationale: The same forecast can have different consequences. Social conditions and available support matter to the review.

## Module 2: Community-led adaptation and resilient services

Outcome: Compare adaptation options with communities, including accessibility, maintenance and unintended consequences.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-032-M2-P1**: Co-design begins while choices can still change; consultation after a fixed design offers limited influence.

- **SWP-032-M2-P2**: An adaptation option may reduce one risk while shifting burdens elsewhere; assess who benefits and who may be harmed.

- **SWP-032-M2-P3**: Accessible services need usable routes, communication and support; a nominal facility is not enough if intended users cannot reach or use it.

- **SWP-032-M2-P4**: Compare adaptation options over their operating life, including responsibility and resources for upkeep.

- **SWP-032-M2-P5**: Combine appropriate technical advice with community knowledge; conflicting accounts should prompt investigation rather than automatic dismissal.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-032-Q06

An adaptation project asks residents for views after the contractor and design are final. What would make the next planning cycle more participatory?

- A. Invite residents only to the opening ceremony
- B. Ask residents to endorse the existing design without changes
- C. Involve residents before options are fixed and show how their input changes decisions **[Key]**
- D. Publish a survey after all funds are spent

Rationale: Input has greater practical value when participants can influence meaningful choices and see the response.

## Module 3: Just transition, advocacy, and accountability

Outcome: Assess how climate decisions distribute benefits and burdens and propose accountable follow-up.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-032-M3-P1**: A transition plan should examine who loses income or access as well as who gains; affected workers and communities need a meaningful role.

- **SWP-032-M3-P2**: An accountability route needs accessible reporting, a responsible responder and a way to track responses without exposing complainants unnecessarily.

- **SWP-032-M3-P3**: Disaggregate benefits where appropriate and safe: an average can hide who is not being reached.

- **SWP-032-M3-P4**: Use evidence that matches the claim and acknowledge uncertainty when communicating with decision-makers.

- **SWP-032-M3-P5**: Review an intervention when evidence shows new burdens; record the concern, consult affected people and use the authorised adjustment process.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-032-Q11

A cleaner-energy programme closes a polluting facility but offers no plan for affected workers. What should a just-transition review add?

- A. A statement that all workers will automatically find new work
- B. A rule preventing workers from raising concerns
- C. Worker and community participation in assessing livelihood impacts and support options **[Key]**
- D. Only the project's emissions estimate

Rationale: Environmental benefits do not erase distributional consequences. Livelihood effects and participation need explicit attention.

Teaching link: SWP-032-M3-P1

#### SWP-032-Q12

Residents can submit climate-project complaints, but nobody is assigned to respond. What is the most important missing element?

- A. A larger complaint box with the same process
- B. A requirement to publish every complainant's name
- C. A limit allowing complaints only on opening day
- D. A clear response owner and a trackable follow-up process **[Key]**

Rationale: Collecting complaints without responsibility for action does not establish an effective accountability route.

Teaching link: SWP-032-M3-P2

#### SWP-032-Q13

A programme reports improved average access to water. Which further check best tests fairness?

- A. Whether improvement reaches groups facing the greatest access barriers **[Key]**
- B. Whether the report contains more project photos
- C. Whether unsuccessful households can be excluded from the denominator
- D. Whether the average can be rounded upward

Rationale: A positive average may coexist with persistent exclusion. Distribution matters alongside the headline result.

#### SWP-032-Q14

A campaign has ten resident interviews about a local drainage issue. Which claim is best supported?

- A. The project is guaranteed to solve all future flooding
- B. These interviews describe concerns that warrant further local investigation **[Key]**
- C. The interviews prove the exact prevalence across the country
- D. Every resident agrees with every interviewee

Rationale: The interviews provide situated accounts, not a national prevalence estimate or a guarantee.

#### SWP-032-Q15

Monitoring shows an adaptation project's fees exclude some intended users. What is the best next step?

- A. Stop recording low-income users
- B. Declare the exclusion unrelated without investigation
- C. Review affordability with affected users and responsible decision-makers, then consider adjustments **[Key]**
- D. Keep the fees unchanged because the launch was successful

Rationale: Monitoring should inform decisions. The observed barrier calls for review rather than removal from the evidence.
