## Module 1: Leadership governance and role clarity

Outcome: Clarify decision authority, conflicts of interest and accountability in programme teams.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-033-M1-P1**: Delegation should state the decision scope, constraints, support and escalation route; it is not unlimited transfer of responsibility.

- **SWP-033-M1-P2**: When personal interests could influence a decision, disclose the conflict and use the organisation's independent decision process.

- **SWP-033-M1-P3**: Record the decision, its reasons, authority, owner and review point; minutes that only say 'agreed' do not establish accountability.

- **SWP-033-M1-P4**: Invite relevant perspectives before decisions and explain how disagreement was handled; inclusion is more than unanimous-looking minutes.

- **SWP-033-M1-P5**: Authority to manage a programme does not automatically confer competence or permission to perform specialist professional tasks.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-033-Q01

A coordinator tells a new worker to 'handle the whole project' without defining authority. What is the first governance improvement?

- A. Assume the worker can sign every contract
- B. Agree which decisions the worker can make and which require approval or escalation **[Key]**
- C. Judge the worker only after the project ends
- D. Remove all supervision to demonstrate trust

Rationale: Clear authority reduces ambiguity and supports appropriate decisions; vague delegation does not establish permission for every action.

## Module 2: Reflective supervision and team support

Outcome: Use structured supervision to support learning, wellbeing and safe escalation.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-033-M2-P1**: A supervision agreement should clarify purpose, roles, regularity, records, confidentiality limits and routes for urgent concerns.

- **SWP-033-M2-P2**: Reflective supervision explores observations, assumptions, emotions and alternatives, then agrees a learning action; it is more than checking task counts.

- **SWP-033-M2-P3**: Supportive supervision does not replace urgent safeguarding or incident procedures; use the appropriate route without waiting for a scheduled session.

- **SWP-033-M2-P4**: Feedback should identify specific behaviour and its effect, invite the worker's perspective, and agree a feasible improvement and review.

- **SWP-033-M2-P5**: Repeated overload is a work-design issue to review as well as an individual support concern; consider case demands, resources and safe priorities.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-033-Q06

A supervisee believes everything said in supervision is absolutely secret. What should the supervisor clarify?

- A. That keeping no record removes every confidentiality issue
- B. The agreement's confidentiality limits and how safety or other reportable concerns are handled **[Key]**
- C. That no concern can ever leave the room
- D. That all discussions will be posted publicly

Rationale: Supervision needs clear expectations. Neither absolute secrecy nor indiscriminate disclosure is an appropriate default.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M2-P1

#### SWP-033-Q07

A worker says, 'I assumed the family was unwilling because they missed two appointments.' Which prompt best encourages reflection?

- A. What evidence supports that view, what alternatives are possible, and how could you check them? **[Key]**
- B. Which colleague can be blamed for the missed appointments?
- C. Can you repeat the assumption in stronger language?
- D. How can you close the case before the end of the day?

Rationale: The prompt separates evidence from interpretation and opens a practical learning step rather than reinforcing an untested judgement.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M2-P2

#### SWP-033-Q08

A worker reports an immediate safeguarding concern shortly before a routine supervision meeting next week. What should the supervisor advise?

- A. Use the applicable urgent safeguarding route now and arrange support alongside it **[Key]**
- B. Save the concern for next week's reflective discussion
- C. Treat a supportive conversation as completion of all required action
- D. Ask the worker to keep it off the record

Rationale: Reflection and support are valuable but do not substitute for timely action through the appropriate safety procedure.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M2-P3

#### SWP-033-Q09

A supervisor wants to address incomplete handovers. Which feedback is most useful?

- A. Discuss a specific missing handover detail, its effect and an agreed improvement **[Key]**
- B. Avoid feedback until the annual review
- C. Compare the worker's personality with a colleague's
- D. Tell the worker they are generally careless

Rationale: Specific, actionable feedback supports learning and follow-up; personal labels do not explain what should change.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M2-P4

#### SWP-033-Q10

Several workers miss breaks and report growing backlogs. What is the best supervisory response?

- A. Offer a wellbeing poster while leaving all demands unchanged
- B. Treat every backlog as proof of poor motivation
- C. Cancel supervision until the backlog disappears
- D. Review workload and resources, agree safe priorities and offer support **[Key]**

Rationale: Support should address the conditions contributing to overload, not only encourage individuals to cope.

## Module 3: Programme management and learning

Outcome: Use delivery information to manage risks, resources and authorised programme adjustments.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-033-M3-P1**: Track an implementation issue with its impact, owner, action and review date; a list without follow-up is not a management response.

- **SWP-033-M3-P2**: A variance from plan should be explained before action: compare intended and actual delivery, investigate causes and consider service effects.

- **SWP-033-M3-P3**: Changes beyond delegated limits require approval; document why a change is proposed and its likely effects before committing resources.

- **SWP-033-M3-P4**: Distinguish a delivery count from a result for participants; both can be useful but they answer different questions.

- **SWP-033-M3-P5**: A learning adjustment should state the change, owner, expected effect and review criteria, while preserving safety and authorised scope.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-033-Q11

A referral partner repeatedly misses agreed handovers. What should the programme lead record and act on?

- A. Only the partner's logo
- B. Only the original launch announcement
- C. A note to discuss it at an unspecified future time
- D. The effect on service continuity, responsible contacts and a dated corrective action **[Key]**

Rationale: A clear owner and follow-up make the issue actionable and connect it to service continuity.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M3-P1

#### SWP-033-Q12

A programme is spending less than planned because accessible transport has not been provided. How should the lead interpret the underspend?

- A. As permission to remove the transport budget permanently
- B. As a possible delivery gap requiring investigation, not automatically as success **[Key]**
- C. As irrelevant because the accounts balance
- D. As proof that the service became more efficient

Rationale: Lower spending may reflect unmet service needs. Financial totals must be interpreted with delivery information.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M3-P2

#### SWP-033-Q13

A coordinator wants to move funds to a new activity outside their delegated authority. What should happen first?

- A. Submit the reason and implications through the authorised change process **[Key]**
- B. Describe the new activity under the old budget label
- C. Ask a supplier to hide the change in an invoice
- D. Commit the money and request approval at year end

Rationale: The proposed change needs proper authority and a transparent record; relabelling does not create permission.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M3-P3

#### SWP-033-Q14

A report says 200 sessions were delivered and therefore every participant's wellbeing improved. What is the problem?

- A. The number is too small to report without doubling it
- B. Delivery and outcomes must always be numerically identical
- C. The session count does not by itself establish a participant outcome **[Key]**
- D. A session count can never be recorded

Rationale: Delivery volume describes activity. The outcome claim needs appropriate evidence beyond the count.

Teaching link: SWP-033-M3-P4

#### SWP-033-Q15

A pilot team changes appointment reminders after feedback. What makes this an accountable learning adjustment?

- A. Remove unsuccessful participants from subsequent reporting
- B. Change several unrelated processes without recording them
- C. Document the change and owner, check risks, and agree how and when to review its effects **[Key]**
- D. Assume every suggestion must be implemented permanently

Rationale: A bounded, documented change can be assessed and revised; unrecorded changes make learning and accountability harder.
