## Module 1: Practice questions and meaningful participation

Outcome: Frame an answerable practice question and plan meaningful, voluntary participation.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-034-M1-P1**: An answerable practice question identifies a specific issue, population or setting and a feasible inquiry; it should not assume its conclusion.

- **SWP-034-M1-P2**: Meaningful participation includes influence over relevant questions and interpretation, with roles and limits agreed in advance.

- **SWP-034-M1-P3**: Separate participation choices from service eligibility; make refusal possible without threatening support, and manage power differences.

- **SWP-034-M1-P4**: Researchers should reflect on how their role and assumptions influence questions, recruitment and interpretation; reflection does not eliminate bias.

- **SWP-034-M1-P5**: Agree co-researcher roles, support, compensation arrangements where applicable, access and confidentiality expectations before tasks begin.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-034-Q01

Which question is best suited to a small inquiry about missed appointments at one service?

- A. What explains every missed appointment in the world?
- B. Why are all people who miss appointments irresponsible?
- C. What barriers do participating service users describe when attending appointments at this service? **[Key]**
- D. How can this team prove its service is perfect?

Rationale: The bounded question can be explored without presuming blame or claiming universal reach.

#### SWP-034-Q02

Residents are invited only to approve a finished research report. What would give them more meaningful influence in the next inquiry?

- A. Replace discussion with a photograph for the report
- B. Agree involvement in framing questions and interpreting findings before collection starts **[Key]**
- C. Invite only residents who already agree with the conclusions
- D. Ask for more signatures on the final cover page

Rationale: Early and substantive roles can influence the inquiry. End-stage endorsement alone provides limited participation.

#### SWP-034-Q03

A service user thinks declining an interview will jeopardise their support. What should the team address before recruitment continues?

- A. Clarify and protect voluntary choice and separate recruitment from service decisions **[Key]**
- B. Ask the caseworker to insist on participation
- C. Use the fear to obtain more responses
- D. Treat receipt of support as automatic research consent

Rationale: The perceived dependency undermines free choice. The recruitment process must address the power relationship.

#### SWP-034-Q04

A worker researching their own service believes its new process must be effective. What should they do with that assumption?

- A. Rewrite questions so every answer supports the process
- B. Treat their job role as proof of objectivity
- C. Record it and seek methods and perspectives that can challenge it **[Key]**
- D. Exclude any account that contradicts it

Rationale: Making the assumption visible and allowing challenge helps manage its influence; it does not guarantee neutrality.

#### SWP-034-Q05

A resident co-researcher is asked to interview neighbours without training or a role agreement. What should happen first?

- A. Require them to disclose neighbours' stories at a public meeting
- B. Assume living locally replaces preparation
- C. Agree the role and boundaries, provide appropriate support and review privacy risks **[Key]**
- D. Give them unrestricted access to all case files

Rationale: Local experience is valuable but does not replace clear responsibilities, preparation and information safeguards.

## Module 2: Ethical inquiry and interpretation

Outcome: Collect proportionate information and interpret findings without overstating what the inquiry can show.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-034-M2-P1**: Choose the minimum information needed for the question and keep recruitment contacts separate from responses where feasible.

- **SWP-034-M2-P2**: Small groups can be identifiable through combinations of details even when names are removed; review quotations and context before sharing.

- **SWP-034-M2-P3**: Recruitment methods shape whose experiences are represented; describe missing perspectives and avoid generalising beyond the sample.

- **SWP-034-M2-P4**: Conflicting findings should be explored and reported transparently, not removed merely to make a simple story.

- **SWP-034-M2-P5**: A before-and-after change alone does not establish that a pilot caused the result; consider other explanations and design limits.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-034-Q06

A satisfaction inquiry proposes collecting identity numbers although its question concerns appointment times. What should the team reconsider?

- A. Whether irrelevant identifiers guarantee truthful answers
- B. Whether identifiers are necessary and whether less identifying information can answer the question **[Key]**
- C. Whether more identifiers would make the report look rigorous
- D. Whether responses can be posted with full identities

Rationale: Identity numbers do not inherently help answer a scheduling question and add avoidable information risk.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M2-P1

#### SWP-034-Q07

A report removes names but describes the only wheelchair user in a small village by job and household details. What is the main risk?

- A. The person may still be identifiable from the combined description **[Key]**
- B. The description cannot be sensitive because it is written in prose
- C. The report becomes automatically anonymous once names are removed
- D. Confidentiality matters only for numeric data

Rationale: Indirect details can identify someone in context. Name removal alone is not a sufficient privacy check.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M2-P2

#### SWP-034-Q08

An online survey reaches only residents who follow the service's social-media page. What should the report acknowledge?

- A. People outside that channel may have different experiences that the survey did not capture **[Key]**
- B. Every resident had an equal chance to respond
- C. The findings prove the views of all non-users
- D. The response count removes any recruitment limitation

Rationale: Channel-based recruitment can miss important groups regardless of the total response count.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M2-P3

#### SWP-034-Q09

Interviews praise a service, but appointment records show persistent drop-out. What is the best next step?

- A. Discard the interviews because numbers always override experience
- B. Explore the difference in what each source measures and report relevant limitations **[Key]**
- C. Average the statements into an unsupported single score
- D. Delete the records because interviews are more positive

Rationale: Different sources can describe different experiences or populations. The discrepancy may be informative.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M2-P4

#### SWP-034-Q10

Attendance rises after a reminder pilot during the same month transport becomes free. What can the team reasonably conclude?

- A. Attendance improved, but the pilot's separate contribution is uncertain **[Key]**
- B. Free transport could not have any influence
- C. The reminder pilot alone caused the entire increase
- D. A before-and-after comparison proves causation in every case

Rationale: Concurrent changes create alternative explanations. The observed improvement is not enough to isolate the reminder's effect.

## Module 3: Responsible innovation and learning

Outcome: Plan a bounded, reviewable service pilot with safeguards and a useful feedback loop.

### Teaching notes

- **SWP-034-M3-P1**: Before a pilot, define the scope, participants, risks, responsible people and criteria for continuing, changing or stopping.

- **SWP-034-M3-P2**: A pilot label does not exempt a project from research-ethics or governance review; obtain the relevant determination before data collection.

- **SWP-034-M3-P3**: Stop or pause criteria should lead to timely review when harms or barriers emerge; completing the pilot schedule is not the only priority.

- **SWP-034-M3-P4**: Record adaptations and their timing so later readers know which version participants experienced.

- **SWP-034-M3-P5**: Return understandable findings to participants, explain limits and decisions, and offer a feasible way to respond.

### Assessment items

#### SWP-034-Q11

A team wants to try a new intake process. Which preparation best supports a responsible pilot?

- A. Define success only after results are known
- B. Remove the current route before checking alternatives
- C. Roll it out everywhere without a review date
- D. Set a limited scope, safeguards, decision criteria and review responsibilities **[Key]**

Rationale: A bounded plan supports a defensible decision and protects participants while uncertainty remains.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M3-P1

#### SWP-034-Q12

A team says ethics review cannot apply because it calls its project 'service innovation'. What should it do?

- A. Begin collecting sensitive data while avoiding any record
- B. Tell participants that pilots have no privacy requirements
- C. Assume the label settles the issue
- D. Ask the relevant governance or ethics body to determine the applicable requirements **[Key]**

Rationale: The activity and its risks, not just its label, determine which review requirements apply.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M3-P2

#### SWP-034-Q13

A new booking pilot repeatedly leaves participants unable to access urgent appointments. What is the best response?

- A. Wait until the scheduled final report before raising the issue
- B. Continue unchanged to obtain a larger sample
- C. Remove those participants from the findings
- D. Use the agreed safety and escalation route and review whether to pause or change the pilot **[Key]**

Rationale: Emerging access risks need timely action. More observations do not justify ignoring the problem.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M3-P3

#### SWP-034-Q14

Halfway through a pilot, staff change the reminder script. What record is needed for interpretation?

- A. Only the best outcome from either version
- B. A combined result that hides the change
- C. What changed, why, when and which participants experienced each version **[Key]**
- D. Only the original script because that was approved first

Rationale: Version and timing information help explain results and prevent attributing every outcome to a single unchanged intervention.

Teaching link: SWP-034-M3-P4

#### SWP-034-Q15

An inquiry ends with a technical report sent only to funders. What would improve accountability to participants?

- A. Assume participants do not want to know the outcome
- B. Share an accessible summary, explain decisions and limitations, and provide a feedback route **[Key]**
- C. Publish identifiable raw interviews to show transparency
- D. Promise every suggestion will be adopted

Rationale: An accessible feedback loop respects participation without promising agreement or exposing private data.
